# CIRCLE

## Story, Now, Next & Possibility

### The temporal and possibility system

---

# 1. Purpose

Circle needs a simple way to understand life across time.

The system therefore organizes meaningful context through four connected concepts:

> **Story → Now → Next → Possibility**

These are not four independent products.

They are four ways of understanding the same underlying reality.

---

# 2. The Fundamental Loop

```text id="snn1a7"
STORY
  ↓
NOW
  ↓
POSSIBILITY
  ↓
NEXT
  ↓
ACTION
  ↓
EXPERIENCE
  ↓
STORY
```

This is one of Circle's primary product loops.

---

# 3. Story

Story represents meaningful things that have happened.

It is the durable memory layer.

Examples:

* a family milestone;
* completed project;
* business achievement;
* community decision;
* important conversation;
* photograph;
* document;
* contribution;
* resolved commitment;
* historical event.

---

# 4. Story Is Selective

Circle must never assume:

> everything that happens deserves to become history.

Story is created through:

### Deliberate preservation

A person explicitly saves something.

### Structured preservation

A system event becomes historically meaningful.

### Formal record

A financial, governance, legal, or operational record requires durable history.

### Automatic candidate

Circle identifies something that may be worth preserving and asks where appropriate.

---

# 5. Story Types

Initial Story types:

```text id="s2p8n4"
MEMORY
MILESTONE
DECISION
ACHIEVEMENT
EVENT
WORK
TRANSACTION
DOCUMENT
MEDIA
RELATIONSHIP
COMMITMENT
PLACE
```

These are classifications, not separate databases.

---

# 6. Story Context

Every Story item should retain relevant context:

```text id="1l6mcv"
WHO
WHAT
WHEN
WHERE
CIRCLE
RELATIONSHIPS
EVIDENCE
MEDIA
RELATED OBJECTS
```

The same Story can therefore be meaningful to multiple people without losing its original context.

---

# 7. Story Connections

A Story item can link to another Story item.

Example:

```text id="q4q4h3"
Family Wedding
     ↓
Birth of Child
     ↓
First Birthday
     ↓
Graduation
```

This creates continuity rather than isolated posts.

---

# 8. Story Visibility

A Story item can have its own visibility.

For example:

```text id="5r2cvb"
Family Story
→ Family only

Business Story
→ Business members

Public Business milestone
→ Public

Private memory
→ Only me
```

Story should never become public merely because it exists.

---

# 9. Now

Now represents current reality.

It is primarily derived from:

* current state;
* active activity;
* current commitments;
* upcoming events;
* current relationships;
* unresolved issues;
* current projects;
* current financial state.

---

# 10. Now Is Not a Feed

The Now surface should answer:

> **What is happening that matters to me?**

It should not answer:

> **What can I endlessly scroll through?**

The system should prioritize:

* relevance;
* urgency;
* importance;
* relationship;
* responsibility.

---

# 11. Now Components

```text id="v0t3fa"
NOW
├── Needs attention
├── Active
├── Upcoming
├── Recently meaningful
└── Quiet context
```

Not all components need to be visible simultaneously.

---

# 12. Now and Attention

Circle should distinguish:

### Important

Matters even if not urgent.

### Urgent

Requires timely action.

### Informational

Useful but does not require action.

### Optional

Interesting but can wait.

This prevents Circle from becoming another notification machine.

---

# 13. Next

Next represents intentional future direction.

It includes:

* plans;
* goals;
* commitments;
* proposals;
* upcoming events;
* future projects;
* desired outcomes.

Next can belong to:

* person;
* Circle;
* project;
* relationship.

---

# 14. Next Has Different Certainty Levels

```text id="w88axp"
IDEA
 ↓
INTENTION
 ↓
PROPOSED
 ↓
AGREED
 ↓
SCHEDULED
 ↓
ACTIVE
 ↓
COMPLETED
 ↓
STORY
```

Not every Next item progresses through every state.

---

# 15. Declared vs Suggested

Circle must distinguish between:

### Declared Next

The user says:

> “I want to start a photography business.”

### Suggested Possibility

Circle says:

> “Your recent work and connections could support exploring photography services.”

The second must never silently become the first.

---

# 16. Possibility

Possibility is the bridge between what exists and what could exist.

It is generated from context.

A Possibility may connect:

* people;
* skills;
* experiences;
* places;
* needs;
* assets;
* relationships;
* previous work;
* stated intentions.

---

# 17. Possibility Types

Initial categories:

### Connection

Someone who could meaningfully connect with someone else.

### Opportunity

A potential economic, professional, or social opening.

### Learning

Something that could expand capability.

### Project

A potential thing to build.

### Experience

A possible event, trip, activity, or memory.

### Contribution

A possible way to help.

### Resource

Something potentially useful.

---

# 18. Possibility Is Not a Recommendation Engine

Circle should avoid:

> “Here are 20 things you should do.”

Instead:

> “Because of X and Y, this may be worth exploring.”

Possibility is designed to **expand agency**, not replace it.

---

# 19. Possibility Provenance

Every generated possibility should have an underlying reason.

Conceptually:

```text id="8q70hz"
POSSIBILITY
│
├── Source
├── Relevant context
├── Relationships involved
├── Evidence
├── Confidence
├── Freshness
└── Suggested next step
```

Example:

> **Possible collaboration**

Because:

* you worked on X;
* Daniel works on Y;
* both projects overlap in Z.

---

# 20. Confidence

Possibilities can have internal confidence levels.

However, confidence must never be confused with truth.

Example:

```text id="6k8s3n"
FACT
"I completed Project X."

INFERENCE
"Project X demonstrates experience with Y."

POSSIBILITY
"You might explore offering Y to another Circle."
```

These are fundamentally different.

---

# 21. Possibility Freshness

Possibilities decay.

A possibility based on old information should become less prominent.

Example:

> A job opening from six months ago should not remain equally relevant.

Therefore possibilities should consider:

* age;
* changed relationships;
* changed goals;
* completed projects;
* changed availability.

---

# 22. Dismissal

A person can dismiss a possibility.

Dismissal should teach the system:

> do not surface this type of possibility again under similar conditions.

Where appropriate, users can explain why.

---

# 23. Save

A possibility can become Next.

```text id="t0n6l0"
Possibility
     ↓
Save
     ↓
Next
```

This is an important boundary.

Circle should not silently convert suggestions into commitments.

---

# 24. Possibility → Action

A person may choose:

* Explore;
* Save;
* Share;
* Connect;
* Create project;
* Create commitment;
* Ignore.

Only explicit action changes state.

---

# 25. Possibility Across Circles

A possibility can originate from one Circle and become useful in another.

Example:

```text id="z5b9pu"
Business Story
      ↓
Skill demonstrated
      ↓
Community need
      ↓
Possibility
      ↓
New Community Project
```

Cross-Circle intelligence must respect permissions.

---

# 26. The Living Portrait

Me combines:

```text id="r8j0yz"
STORY
+
NOW
+
NEXT
+
POSSIBILITIES
```

This creates the person's Living Portrait.

It should answer:

> Where have I been?

> Where am I?

> Where am I going?

> What might be possible?

---

# 27. Circle Living Portrait

The same model applies to Circles.

A Business can have:

* Business Story;
* Business Now;
* Business Next;
* Business Possibilities.

A Family can have:

* Family Story;
* Family Now;
* Family Next;
* Family Possibilities.

This makes the temporal model universal.

---

# 28. The Shared Future

Some Next objects belong to multiple people.

Example:

```text id="91q5sm"
Amina
  \
   → Wedding
  /
Daniel
```

The Event belongs to a shared context.

Each person may still have different personal views and responsibilities.

---

# 29. Past / Present / Future as Architecture

The original conceptual model remains valuable:

> Past / Present / Future

But the product vocabulary should be:

> **Story / Now / Next**

This preserves conceptual depth while making the interface ordinary and understandable.

---

# 30. The Temporal Contract

Every meaningful object should be able to answer:

> What happened?

> What is true now?

> What happens next?

Not every object needs all three.

But the system should support the relationship.

---

# 31. Final Model

```text id="5trj6x"
                 POSSIBILITY
                ↗           ↘
           STORY             NEXT
              ↑               ↓
              └──── NOW ──────┘
                    │
                  ACTION
                    │
                EXPERIENCE
                    │
                    ↓
                  STORY
```

Circle becomes more valuable as this loop becomes richer while remaining understandable.
