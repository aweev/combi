# CIRCLE

## Master Product Requirements Document

### Definitive Product Foundation & Product Direction

**Status:** Master / Replacement Draft
**Product:** Circle
**Core principle:** Keep Your Circle Tight. Expand What’s Possible.

---

# 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Circle is a personal and collective system for organizing the people, relationships, moments, activities, plans, resources, and possibilities that matter.

It is not primarily a social network, productivity application, CRM, ERP, family app, community platform, or business-management tool.

It is a **shared foundation** on which all of those contexts can exist when needed.

Circle begins with a simple idea:

> **People live in relationships and contexts. Those relationships have a past, a present, a future, and possibilities.**

Circle gives those relationships a place to exist and evolve.

The fundamental experience is:

> **People → Relationships → Circles → Story → Now → Next → Possibilities → Action → Story**

The product should feel simple even though the system underneath is highly interconnected.

---

# 2. THE PRODUCT IN ONE SENTENCE

> **Circle helps you keep what matters close, understand where you are, remember where you've been, shape what's next, and discover what could be possible.**

---

# 3. THE PRODUCT PROMISE

Circle should answer five fundamental human questions:

### Who matters to me?

**People**

### Where do we belong together?

**Circles**

### What has happened?

**Story**

### What is happening now?

**Now**

### What could happen next?

**Next + Possibilities**

Everything else exists to make these questions easier to answer and act upon.

---

# 4. THE CENTRAL PHILOSOPHY

## Keep Your Circle Tight.

Circle is built around the belief that meaningful life is not defined by the largest possible audience.

It is shaped by:

* people who matter;
* relationships that matter;
* contexts that matter;
* information that matters;
* experiences that matter;
* possibilities worth exploring.

Therefore Circle should optimize for:

> **meaningful connection over maximum reach.**

And:

> **continuity over constant engagement.**

And:

> **usefulness over noise.**

---

# 5. THE SECOND HALF OF THE PHILOSOPHY

## Expand What’s Possible.

Keeping your Circle tight does not mean keeping your world small.

The system should help users recognize:

* useful connections;
* overlooked resources;
* relevant people;
* opportunities;
* patterns;
* ideas;
* potential projects;
* potential experiences;
* potential next steps.

This becomes the role of the:

> **Possibility Expander**

---

# 6. WHAT CIRCLE IS NOT

Circle should deliberately resist becoming:

* another follower-based social network;
* another infinite feed;
* another generic chat application;
* another CRM;
* another ERP;
* another project-management application;
* another family-only application;
* another community-only application;
* another generic AI assistant;
* another collection of disconnected productivity tools.

Circle may eventually contain capabilities found in some of these products.

But those capabilities exist **inside the Circle foundation**, rather than defining the product.

* identity;

* Next;

* permissions.

# 9. FACE

The Face is not merely a profile.

It is the person's:

> **living front door into Circle.**

It can represent:

* who they are;
* what they choose to show;
* their Circles;
* selected Story;
* current context;
* selected Next;
* selected Possibilities;
* relevant connections.

The Face should feel alive without becoming a social-media profile.

# 10. CIRCLE

A Circle is a meaningful shared context.

A Circle can represent:

* family;
* business;
* fellowship;
* community;
* association;
* project group;
* team;
* organization;
* or another meaningful collective.

The Circle itself is the foundation.

The archetype describes the context.

# 11. THE SMALLEST COMPLETE CIRCLE

A Circle does not need dozens of modules to be useful.

The smallest complete Circle contains:

```text
PEOPLE
STORY
NOW
NEXT
POSSIBILITIES
```

Everything else is optional.

This is one of the most important simplifications in the architecture.

---

# 12. EVERY CIRCLE HAS THE SAME TEMPORAL MODEL

The Past / Present / Future model is universal.

For a person:

> My Story → My Now → My Next → My Possibilities

For a Family:

> Our Story → Our Now → Our Next → Our Possibilities

For a Business:

> Our Story → Our Now → Our Next → Our Possibilities

For a Fellowship:

> Our Story → Our Now → Our Next → Our Possibilities

For a Community:

> Our Story → Our Now → Our Next → Our Possibilities

This creates one mental model throughout the product.

---

# 13. STORY

The previous concept of **Chronicle** is replaced in the user-facing product by:

> **Story**

Story is the historical and meaningful record of a person or Circle.

It can contain:

* memories;
* events;
* milestones;
* achievements;
* media;
* projects;
* decisions;
* important moments;
* meaningful conversations;
* historical records.

Story is not simply a chronological feed.

It is:

> **the meaningful history of a Circle.**

---

# 14. NOW

Now represents the present.

It answers:

> **What is happening?**

It can surface:

* current activity;
* active commitments;
* current projects;
* upcoming events;
* requests;
* important updates;
* things requiring attention.

Now should prioritize meaning and relevance rather than raw chronology.

---

# 15. NEXT

Next represents direction.

It answers:

> **What are we moving toward?**

It can contain:

* plans;
* commitments;
* goals;
* projects;
* upcoming events;
* intentions;
* planned actions.

Next is where possibility becomes deliberate direction.

---

# 16. POSSIBILITIES

Possibilities answer:

> **What could happen?**

They may include:

* people to connect with;
* projects to consider;
* experiences;
* resources;
* opportunities;
* ideas;
* capabilities;
* potential relationships;
* possible next steps.

A possibility is not an instruction.

It is an invitation to explore.

---

# 17. THE POSSIBILITY EXPANDER

The Possibility Expander is the intelligence layer of Circle.

It looks across authorized context and helps users see connections they may not have noticed.

It can operate at every level.

### Me

> What could be possible for me?

### Family

> What could we do together?

### Business

> What could we build next?

### Fellowship

> What could we experience together?

### Community

> What could we create together?

---

# 18. POSSIBILITY PRINCIPLE

Circle should:

> **expand what people can see, not dictate what they should choose.**

The system may suggest.

The person decides.

The preferred flow is:

```text
Possibility
↓
Explore
↓
Consider
↓
Choose
↓
Next
↓
Action
↓
Story
```

The person's own world.

### Business

A commercial or professional organization.

A broader collective context.

Potential capabilities include:

* Events;
* Projects;
* Messaging;
* Documents;

* Finance;
* Inventory;
* Customers;
* Forms;
* Membership;

# 22. CAPABILITY ACTIVATION

A new Circle should not overwhelm users with every possible capability.

Capabilities should appear:

* when relevant;
* when enabled;
* when the archetype suggests them;
* when the user requests them;
* when Circle learns that they are useful.

The system should progressively reveal complexity.

---

# 23. THE RULE FOR NEW FEATURES

Any proposed feature should be tested against:

> Does this naturally connect People, Circle, Story, Now, Next, or Possibilities?

If not, the feature should be questioned before being added.

This becomes a permanent product filter.

# 24. RELATIONSHIPS

Relationships are fundamental infrastructure.

Circle should model relationships between:

* Person ↔ Person;
* Person ↔ Circle;
* Circle ↔ Circle;
* Person ↔ Object;
* Circle ↔ Object.

Relationships should have:

* type;
* context;
* permissions;
* status;
* provenance;
* lifecycle.

---

# 25. CONTEXT IS CRITICAL

The same person can have different relationships in different Circles.

For example:

> someone may be a Family member in one context and a Business partner in another.

Circle must not flatten these relationships into one universal social graph.

---

# 26. MEMBERSHIP

Membership connects a person to a Circle.

Membership may include:

* role;
* permissions;
* status;
* invitation state;
* date joined;
* date departed.

---

# 27. ROLES

Roles are contextual.

A person may be:

> Admin

in one Circle,

and:

> Member

in another.

Roles should not define the entire person's identity.

```text

ROLE

CAPABILITY
```

# 29. PRIVACY

Privacy should be contextual rather than binary.

Possible visibility levels include:

* Only Me;
* Selected People;
* Relationship;
* Circle;
* Connected Circles;
* Public.

An object's visibility should never be assumed merely because its creator belongs to a Circle.

# 30. COMMUNICATION

Circle supports communication, but communication must retain context.

Communication can occur:

* person-to-person;
* within a Circle;
* around an Event;
* around a Project;
* around a Story;
* around another object.

The goal is:

> **communication with continuity.**

---

# 31. MESSAGE → ACTION

Communication may become:

* Commitment;
* Event;
* Project;
* Story;
* Next;
* Possibility.

But Circle should normally suggest the transition rather than silently perform it.

---

# 32. ACTIVITY ≠ MESSAGE

A message means:

> someone communicated.

Activity means:

> something happened.

These should remain distinct.

---

# 33. NOTIFICATIONS

Notifications exist to bring attention to meaningful events.

They should not exist primarily to increase app opens.

Circle should favor:

> fewer meaningful notifications

over:

> maximum engagement.

Notifications should support:

* grouping;
* priority;
* quiet hours;
* muting;
* digest;
* direct actions.

> What might I not know exists?

# 36. CROSS-CIRCLE DISCOVERY

For example:

```text

+
```

However:

> discovery must never automatically create a relationship or expose private information.

# 37. KEEP YOUR CIRCLE TIGHT

This principle governs discovery.

Circle should not ask:

> "Who can we expose you to?"

It should ask:

> **"What connection is actually meaningful here?"**

# 58. ONE SOURCE OF TRUTH

The architecture should follow:

> **One source of truth, many contextual views.**

A Story referenced by several Circles should not become several unrelated copies.

---

# 59. MODULAR FOUNDATION

Circle should initially favor a modular architecture rather than prematurely becoming a distributed collection of microservices.

Domains should be separated logically before infrastructure is separated physically.

---

# 60. DATA SECURITY

The data layer must enforce authorization in addition to application logic where supported.

Security must not depend solely on the front end.

---

# 61. MIGRATIONS

Database changes must be versioned and dependency-aware.

Functions, policies, tables, triggers, indexes, and relationships must be considered together when changing schema.

Authorization helpers should avoid creating brittle dependency chains.

---

# 63. MEDIA & DOCUMENTS

Large media and documents should use appropriate object storage while the relational system stores:

* metadata;
* permissions;
* relationships;
* lifecycle;

# 64. OFFLINE / LOW-CONNECTIVITY SUPPORT

Circle should eventually support resilient experiences for unreliable connectivity, including:

* drafts;
* retry;
* upload recovery;
* local caching;
* synchronization.

This is particularly relevant to the intended African market context.

# 65. AI

AI is an assistant layer, not the owner of the user's world.

AI should:

* respect permissions;
* explain important suggestions;
* preserve provenance;
* avoid inventing relationships;
* avoid silently modifying important records;
* ask for confirmation for consequential actions.

> possibility

from:

> fact.

# 72. PROGRESSIVE CAPACITY PRICING

The planned economic model uses progressive capacity bands.

Conceptually:

```text
FIRST 1,000,000
→ base price

NEXT 1,000,000
→ higher price

NEXT 1,000,000
→ higher again

...

CAP
→ maximum price
```

The price increases by the defined progression until the capped amount is reached.

The exact numerical pricing schedule belongs in the dedicated Monetization & Economics specification.

---

# 73. WHAT THE CAP REPRESENTS

The pricing system must clearly define whether the counted unit is:

* person;
* account;
* Circle;
* seat;
* capacity allocation;
* or another measurable unit.

The billing architecture must never ambiguously mix these concepts.

# 75. THREE-MONTH INACTIVE SUBSCRIPTION RULE

The planned lifecycle is:

```text
SUBSCRIPTION ENDS
↓
INACTIVE PERIOD
↓
3 MONTHS
↓
ACCOUNT REMOVAL
↓
CAPACITY RELEASED
```

Users should receive appropriate warnings before removal.

The system should distinguish:

> inactive subscription

from:

> deleted account.

---

# 76. CAPACITY RECLAMATION

When an account is removed, the relevant capacity should become available again where the pricing model requires it.

This must be tracked independently from the user's underlying identity.

---

# 77. RETURNING USERS

If someone returns after their account has been removed:

> their previous position/capacity should not automatically be reserved.

If capacity is already occupied, the returning account joins the applicable long list / waitlist.

This prevents permanent reservation of scarce capacity by inactive accounts.

---

# 78. FAIRNESS OF CAPACITY

The lifecycle should prevent inactive accounts from permanently consuming scarce platform capacity.

This is important both economically and technically.

---

# 104. THE ARCHITECTURAL TEST

Before approving a major feature, ask:

1. What Circle does it belong to?
2. Which people does it involve?
3. Does it relate to Story, Now, Next, or Possibilities?
4. Is it a core concept or merely a capability?
5. Does it require a new object?
6. Can an existing object represent it?
7. Does it make Circle simpler or more complicated?
8. Does it strengthen or weaken Keep Your Circle Tight?
9. Does it expand possibility without taking away agency?
10. Could the user understand it without knowing our architecture?

If the answers are unclear, the feature should not automatically be added.
