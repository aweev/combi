# CIRCLE

## Search, Discovery & Possibility Expansion

### Turning Circle's interconnected world into useful discovery

---

# 1. Purpose

Circle will eventually contain:

* people;
* Circles;
* Story;
* relationships;
* events;
* projects;
* documents;
* objects;
* transactions;
* capabilities;
* possibilities.

Search and discovery make this connected world usable.

---

# 2. Search vs Discovery

These are different.

### Search

> **I know roughly what I'm looking for.**

### Discovery

> **I don't know what I'm looking for yet.**

Circle should support both.

---

# 3. Universal Search

Search should operate across the user's authorized world.

Possible results:

```text id="w2f8c6"
PEOPLE
CIRCLES
STORY
EVENTS
PROJECTS
DOCUMENTS
OBJECTS
MESSAGES
TRANSACTIONS
POSSIBILITIES
```

---

# 4. Context-Aware Search

Search should understand current context.

Inside:

> Family

searching:

> "school"

should prioritize Family-related information.

Global search can still search everything the user is authorized to access.

---

# 5. Search Intent

Circle should eventually understand intent.

For example:

> "photos from our trip"

may refer to:

* Story;
* media;
* event;
* Fellowship.

The system should surface the relevant objects rather than requiring exact keywords.

---

# 6. Search Results

Results should be grouped by meaning.

Example:

```text id="r5x2m8"
Family Trip — Limbe

Story
  24 photos

Event
  March 2026

People
  8 participants

Documents
  itinerary.pdf
```

---

# 7. Relationship-Aware Search

Search can use relationship context.

Example:

> "projects with Daniel"

can search:

```text id="m6c8j1"
Daniel
 ↓
Relationship
 ↓
Business
 ↓
Projects
```

---

# 8. Permission-Aware Search

Search must never reveal objects the user cannot access.

This applies to:

* titles;
* snippets;
* metadata;
* people;
* documents.

Even knowing that something exists can sometimes be sensitive.

---

# 9. Search History

Users may see recent searches.

They should be able to clear them.

Search history should not silently become social data.

---

# 10. Saved Searches

Advanced users may save useful searches.

Examples:

> unpaid invoices;

> family events;

> active projects;

> documents expiring soon.

---

# 11. Discovery

Discovery begins when Circle sees meaningful connections among authorized information.

Example:

> You have three people in different Circles who work in renewable energy.

Circle could surface:

> **Possible connection**

---

# 12. Discovery Must Be Contextual

Not every connection should be surfaced.

The system should consider:

* relevance;
* relationship;
* timing;
* user intent;
* privacy;
* confidence.

---

# 13. Possibility Engine

The Possibility Engine is the bridge between:

> what exists

and:

> what could exist.

---

# 14. Possibility Inputs

Potential inputs include:

```text id="8c2n4m"
PERSON
CIRCLE
RELATIONSHIPS
STORY
NOW
NEXT
CAPABILITIES
OBJECTS
EVENTS
ACTIVITY
```

---

# 15. Possibility Output

A possibility might be:

> "You could collaborate with Sarah on the photography project."

or:

> "Your Fellowship could turn the upcoming trip into a recurring annual event."

or:

> "This unused community resource could support the new project."

---

# 16. Possibility Is Not Prediction

Circle should distinguish:

> possible

from:

> likely.

Possibility should not pretend to know the future.

---

# 17. Possibility Provenance

Every meaningful Possibility should be able to explain:

> **Why am I seeing this?**

Example:

> "Because you and Daniel are both working on community photography projects and have previously collaborated."

This builds trust.

---

# 18. Possibility Confidence

The system may internally assess confidence.

But users should not be overwhelmed by artificial scores.

Prefer:

> **Why this might be useful**

over:

> **87% probability of success.**

---

# 19. Possibility Categories

Possibilities may include:

### People

Potential relationships.

### Projects

Potential initiatives.

### Places

Potential destinations.

### Skills

Potential learning.

### Resources

Potentially useful objects.

### Business

Potential opportunities.

### Events

Potential experiences.

### Connections

Potential introductions.

---

# 20. Possibility → Next

When a user chooses:

> Explore

the possibility becomes an exploration.

When they choose:

> Do this

it can become Next.

The system must not skip user consent.

---

# 21. Possibility → Story

A possibility that becomes real can eventually become Story.

Example:

```text id="x5q7r3"
Possibility
 ↓
Explored
 ↓
Next
 ↓
Completed
 ↓
Story
```

This creates a powerful continuity loop.

---

# 22. Possibility → Circle

A possibility may create a new Circle.

Example:

> "You and three friends are planning a trip."

Circle can suggest:

> **Create a Fellowship for the trip?**

---

# 23. Possibility → Relationship

A potential connection can become an actual relationship.

Again:

> suggestion → user decision → relationship.

---

# 24. Possibility → Capability

Circle may recognize:

> "You repeatedly manage stock for this Business."

and suggest:

> **Enable Inventory?**

This is an important mechanism for growing Circle without overwhelming users with modules.

---

# 25. Discovery and Privacy

The Possibility Engine must never exploit private information in inappropriate ways.

It should respect:

* visibility;
* relationship boundaries;
* consent;
* Circle membership;
* sensitive contexts.

---

# 26. Cross-Circle Discovery

This is one of Circle's most powerful possibilities.

A person may have:

```text id="a8m2v5"
Family
Business
Fellowship
Community
```

The system can identify useful intersections.

Example:

> a Family member has expertise that may help the Business.

But it must never assume:

> family relationship = business availability.

---

# 27. Controlled Bridges

Cross-Circle suggestions should be explicit:

> "You might want to introduce them."

not:

> "We've connected them."

---

# 28. Discovery Surface

Possibilities can appear in:

* Home;
* Now;
* Next;
* Circle Home;
* Search;
* Face.

But they should never overwhelm the core interface.

---

# 29. Possibility Inbox

A dedicated area can eventually collect:

> things worth exploring.

Possible states:

```text id="n7h2c5"
NEW
↓
EXPLORE
↓
SAVE
↓
DISMISS
↓
TURN INTO NEXT
↓
COMPLETE
↓
STORY
```

---

# 30. Dismissal

Users should easily say:

> Not relevant.

The system should learn from that without becoming intrusive.

---

# 31. Exploration

A Possibility can open into an exploration space.

Example:

> "Could this Business expand into a new market?"

Circle might organize:

* relevant people;
* resources;
* prior Story;
* possible actions;
* documents;
* risks;
* next steps.

The user remains the decision-maker.

---

# 32. Possibility Expander

The Possibility Expander can eventually become a defining Circle experience.

Its purpose is:

> **help people see options that their current context makes possible.**

Not:

> tell people what they should do.

---

# 33. Personal Possibility Expander

On the Face:

> **What could be possible for me?**

It can use:

* Story;
* current interests;
* relationships;
* Next;
* capabilities.

---

# 34. Family Possibility Expander

Within Family:

> **What could we do together?**

Examples:

* reunion;
* shared project;
* preserving history;
* property planning;
* family business.

---

# 35. Business Possibility Expander

Within Business:

> **What could this Business do next?**

Examples:

* new product;
* partnership;
* new market;
* process improvement;
* unused asset.

---

# 36. Community Possibility Expander

Within Community:

> **What could we build together?**

Examples:

* project;
* partnership;
* event;
* shared resource;
* local initiative.

---

# 37. Fellowship Possibility Expander

Within Fellowship:

> **What could we experience or create together?**

Examples:

* trip;
* event;
* project;
* collaboration;
* tradition.

---

# 38. Discovery Without Addiction

Circle must avoid:

* infinite recommendation feeds;
* algorithmic outrage;
* artificial scarcity;
* engagement traps.

Discovery should help people act meaningfully.

---

# 39. Serendipity

Circle should preserve some room for unexpected discovery.

Example:

> "You may not have known this person is also interested in photography."

But unexpected does not mean random.

It should have contextual justification.

---

# 40. External Discovery

Circle may eventually connect to external information sources.

Examples:

* public places;
* businesses;
* events;
* learning resources;
* services.

External information should be clearly distinguished from Circle's own data.

---

# 41. Search + AI

AI can improve:

* semantic search;
* summarization;
* relationship understanding;
* possibility generation;
* natural-language queries.

But AI should not silently modify records.

---

# 42. AI Actions

When AI proposes an action:

> **suggest first.**

Examples:

> "I found three related Stories. Merge them?"

> "Would you like to create a Project from this conversation?"

> "You may want to invite these people."

The user decides.

---

# 43. Explainability

Important AI-driven discoveries should provide:

> **Why this?**

with enough context to make the suggestion understandable.

---

# 44. Feedback

Users can provide:

* useful;
* not useful;
* incorrect;
* irrelevant;
* don't show this again.

This helps improve discovery.

---

# 45. Discovery Safety

The system must be conservative where incorrect connections could cause harm.

It should avoid confidently inferring:

* sensitive personal traits;
* private relationships;
* hidden intentions;
* highly sensitive circumstances.

---

# 46. The Discovery Loop

The overall Circle loop becomes:

```text id="e4n7w3"
WHAT EXISTS
     ↓
UNDERSTAND CONTEXT
     ↓
DISCOVER CONNECTION
     ↓
SURFACE POSSIBILITY
     ↓
USER EXPLORES
     ↓

     ↓
ACTION
     ↓
STORY
     ↓
NEW CONTEXT
```

This is the deeper engine of Circle.

# 49. The Possibility Principle

> **Circle should expand what people can see, not dictate what they should choose.**

---

# 50. Final Principle

Search helps people find:

> **what they already know exists.**

Discovery helps people notice:

> **what they did not know was connected.**

Possibility helps them explore:

> **what could exist next.**

Together, these become one of Circle's most important differentiators.
