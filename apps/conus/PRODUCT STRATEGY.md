# CONUS PRODUCT STRATEGY

## Product definition, behavior system, user journeys, and product boundaries

**Version 1.0**

---

# 00 — THE ROLE OF THIS DOCUMENT

The CONUS Constitution defines **why CONUS exists**.

The CONUS Operating Bible defines **how the CONUS system operates**.

The CONUS Zero Blueprint defines **what CONUS must prove first**.

This document defines:

> **What the CONUS product actually is.**

It translates the larger CONUS vision into a coherent product system before software requirements are written.

The PRD comes after this document.

The Product Strategy should answer:

* Who is CONUS for?
* What problem does it solve?
* What behavior does it create?
* What does a person actually do inside it?
* What does a creator get from it?
* What does a business get from it?
* What makes the product different?
* What systems must exist?
* What belongs in CONUS Zero?
* What belongs later?
* What must CONUS never become?

---

# 01 — THE PRODUCT THESIS

CONUS is an **entertainment and participation platform** where:

> **Creators make things people want to experience.**

> **CONUS curates and develops those experiences.**

> **Audiences participate and generate demand signals.**

> **Communities amplify what matters to them.**

> **Brands fund experiences that create meaningful attention.**

> **Recognition turns attention into opportunity.**

The product therefore sits at the intersection of:

**Entertainment**

**Participation**

**Discovery**

**Competition**

**Community**

**Recognition**

**Opportunity**

**Commercial value**

CONUS is not primarily a content library.

It is not primarily a social network.

It is not primarily a quiz application.

It is not primarily an advertising platform.

It is a system that connects all of these behaviors into one loop.

---

# 02 — THE CORE PRODUCT LOOP

The fundamental CONUS loop is:

```text
DISCOVER
   ↓
EXPERIENCE
   ↓
PARTICIPATE
   ↓
SIGNAL
   ↓
RECOGNIZE
   ↓
CREATE OPPORTUNITY
   ↓
RETURN
```

A person discovers something.

They experience it.

They participate.

Their behavior creates a signal.

CONUS recognizes what emerges.

That recognition can create opportunity.

The resulting experience gives people a reason to return.

This loop is the heart of the product.

# 07 — THE CONUS USER SYSTEM

CONUS has several user roles.

These are roles within the system, not necessarily separate account types.

## 1. FAN

Consumes and participates.

## 2. CREATOR

Creates experiences and ideas.

## 3. CONTESTANT

Participates in competitions.

## 4. FOUNDER

Presents a business, skill, idea, service, or capability.

## 5. COMMUNITY MEMBER

Participates through geographic or cultural identity.

## 6. PARTNER / SPONSOR

Funds or supports experiences.

## 7. CONUS TEAM

Creates, curates, moderates, manages, measures, and operates the system.

A single person can occupy multiple roles.

A fan can become a creator.

A creator can become a founder.

A contestant can become a star.

A fan can become a champion.

A creator can become a CONUS Original.

This fluidity is important.

### ENGINE

A behavior CONUS wants to create.

* habit
* watching
* participation
* competition
* discovery
* social sharing
* community
* opportunity

### PROPERTY

A recognizable product experience that activates an engine.

For example:

**Habit Engine**

→ Daily CONUS

**Creator Engine**

→ CONUS Pitch

**Competition Engine**

→ CONUS Challenge

**Opportunity Engine**

→ CONUS Founders

**Live Engine**

→ CONUS Live

The product architecture should preserve this distinction.

# 20 — COMPETITION

Competition exists to create:

* anticipation
* participation
* progress
* identity
* recognition

It is not the entire identity of CONUS.

Competition should be:

* understandable
* fair
* transparent
* time-bound
* meaningful
* resistant to manipulation

The product must avoid becoming:

> **a popularity contest disguised as merit.**

Where competition depends on skill, performance, or contribution, those signals should be visible.

---

# 21 — VOTING

Voting is one of the strongest audience participation mechanisms.

But voting must have clear rules.

Every voting experience should define:

* who can vote
* how many votes are allowed
* voting period
* eligibility
* whether votes are weighted
* fraud controls
* result verification
* result publication

Audience choice should matter.

But the system should not pretend that every question can be reduced to a vote.

---

# 22 — LEADERBOARDS

### LOCAL

### PROPERTY

How am I doing within this competition?

### TIME

Today / week / season.

The product should prioritize meaningful rankings over endless numerical competition.

# 54 — THE GREENLIGHT MODEL

A CONUS idea can be evaluated through several signals:

### CREATIVE

Is the idea compelling?

### AUDIENCE

Do people respond?

### PARTICIPATION

Do people do something with it?

### RETENTION

Do they return?

### SOCIAL

Do they share?

### COMMERCIAL

Can it support a sustainable business relationship?

### OPERATIONAL

Can CONUS produce it repeatedly?

---

# 55 — THE PRODUCT DECISION FRAMEWORK

For every new feature, ask:

### 1. Does it help discovery?

### 2. Does it improve the experience?

### 3. Does it create meaningful participation?

### 4. Does it generate a useful signal?

### 5. Does it create recognition?

### 6. Does it create opportunity?

### 7. Does it improve the commercial system?

If it does none of these:

> **Do not build it.**

The product should earn complexity.

# 69 — THE PRODUCT PRINCIPLE OF ZERO

During Zero:

> **Manual before automated.**

If a process can be done manually while learning, do it manually.

Example:

Instead of building a sponsor dashboard:

**team manages sponsor campaign manually.**

Instead of building an advanced creator marketplace:

**team reviews submissions manually.**

Instead of sophisticated recommendations:

**editorial team curates discovery.**

Automation comes after the behavior is proven.

# 70 — THE PRODUCT PRINCIPLE OF SCALE

Automate only when:

1. the behavior is proven,
2. the process repeats,
3. the manual workload is measurable,
4. automation creates meaningful efficiency.

This prevents CONUS from building infrastructure around assumptions.
