# CONUS EXPERIENCE / UX BLUEPRINT

## 01. Purpose

The CONUS UX Blueprint defines how CONUS should feel and behave from the moment someone encounters it to the moment they become a recurring participant, creator, recognized community member, or commercial partner.

This is not a visual design document.

It defines:

* user experience principles
* behavioral loops
* user states
* key journeys
* interaction patterns
* information hierarchy
* participation mechanics
* discovery behavior
* competition behavior
* recognition behavior
* local behavior
* creator and founder experiences
* reward experiences
* sharing behavior
* conversion moments
* trust requirements
* failure states
* UX requirements for CONUS Zero

The central UX question is:

> **What should a person naturally want to do next?**

CONUS should never feel like a collection of features.

It should feel like a living system where every meaningful action leads naturally to another meaningful action.

---

# 02. The CONUS Experience Model

The core experience is:

**DISCOVER → EXPERIENCE → PARTICIPATE → SIGNAL → RECOGNIZE → CREATE OPPORTUNITY → REPEAT**

This is the behavioral spine of the product.

A person might enter through a story.

They watch.

They become curious.

They answer a question.

They receive a result.

They see their position.

They share it.

Someone follows their result.

They discover another challenge.

They return tomorrow.

Eventually they become recognized.

Eventually they may create, compete, win, or receive an opportunity.

The UX must make this progression feel natural.

---

# 03. The First Principle

## CONUS should never ask:

> “What feature do you want to use?”

It should ask:

> **“What is happening right now?”**

Examples:

* Today's CONUS is live.
* A new episode dropped.
* A challenge closes tonight.
* Your city is competing.
* Voting is open.
* Someone just became Fan of the Week.
* A creator has entered the Pitch.
* A Founder needs your vote.
* A new reward is available.
* A live event is starting.

The product should feel **alive**.

---

# 04. Experience Personality

CONUS should feel like a combination of:

### Magazine

Because there is culture, editorial judgment, stories, people, topics, and discovery.

### Game show

Because there are moments of anticipation, participation, competition, reveals, and winners.

### Social feed

Because people, reactions, shares, conversations, and identity matter.

### Sports league

Because rankings, momentum, rivalries, seasons, local pride, and records create continuity.

### Entertainment network

Because CONUS produces and distributes original entertainment.

These are reference behaviors, not interfaces to copy literally.

# 05. UX Principles

## 5.1 Show, don't explain

Whenever possible, the product should demonstrate what CONUS is rather than explaining it.

Instead of:

> “CONUS is an entertainment and participation platform…”

Show:

> **TODAY'S CONUS**
>
> Watch.
>
> Play.
>
> See where you rank.

The experience teaches the product.

---

## 5.2 One obvious next action

Every important screen should have a clear primary action.

Examples:

Story:

**Watch**

Episode:

**Continue**

Challenge:

**Enter Challenge**

Quiz:

**Start**

Voting:

**Vote**

Creator:

**Follow**

Founder:

**Support**

Result:

**Share**

Leaderboard:

**See Who's Ahead**

The interface should not force people to interpret what they are supposed to do.

# 6. The CONUS Home Experience

Home is not a directory.

Home is the **current state of CONUS**.

The first screen should answer five questions quickly:

1. What is happening?
2. Why should I care?
3. What can I do?
4. What is changing?
5. What should I do next?

---

# 7. The New User Experience

The first visit should not require registration before value is demonstrated.

A new visitor should be able to:

* see what is happening
* watch something
* explore
* participate where possible
* see results
* understand the competitive layer
* understand the community layer

Only then should CONUS introduce account creation when it provides a clear benefit.

For example:

> **Want to keep your streak?**
>
> Create your CONUS profile.

Or:

> **Want your result saved?**
>
> Join CONUS.

This makes registration a consequence of value rather than a barrier to discovering value.

* a story
* challenge
* quiz
* person
* creator

* trend

## Stage 4 — Feedback

The product immediately responds.

Examples:

* score

* progress
* recognition

The person sees:

or:

> You beat 72% of participants.

or:

> Your city is currently #2.

---

> **Want another one?**

or:

> **Your city needs you.**

or:

> **Episode 2 is waiting.**

or:

> **Voting closes tonight.**

After enough activity:

> Welcome back.

The person now has:

* points
* history
* streak
* rank
* badges
* followed creators
* followed shows
* local identity
* participation history

They are no longer just a visitor.

# 9. The Participation Loop

The ideal participation experience is:

**PROMPT → ACTION → FEEDBACK → POSITION → NEXT ACTION**

Example:

> **Today's Challenge**
>
> Who knows this song?

↓

Answer.

↓

> **Correct.**

↓

> **8,421 people answered.**
>
> You ranked **#214**.

↓

> **Your city is currently #3.**

↓

> **Help Kumba move up.**

↓

Next action.

This is more powerful than simply displaying:

> “You scored 8/10.”

The result must exist inside a larger system.

---

# 10. The Quiz Experience

The quiz should feel fast and intentional.

## Before starting

Show:

* topic
* number of questions
* time limit
* reward
* eligibility
* competition context

Example:

> **TODAY'S CONUS**
>
> Cameroonian Music
>
> 5 Questions
> 60 Seconds
>
> **Compete for today's reward**
>
> [START]

---

## During quiz

Minimize distractions.

Priorities:

1. Question
2. Answers
3. Progress
4. Timer

Avoid unnecessary navigation.

---

## After each answer

Feedback may be immediate or deferred depending on competition design.

The system should make the participant understand:

* whether they answered
* whether they are progressing
* how much time remains

---

## Completion

The completion moment should feel significant.

Show:

* score
* percentile/rank
* local position
* national position where relevant
* points
* streak
* reward eligibility
* relevant achievement

Then provide a next action.

---

# 11. Results Are Content

A result should not be treated as a dead end.

It is a **shareable object**.

Example:

> **I scored 9/10 on today's CONUS.**
>
> #214 nationally
> #7 in Kumba

Then:

**Share Result**

The shared result should lead directly back to CONUS.

This creates a distribution loop:

**Participation → Result → Share → New Visitor → Participation**

# 17. The Creator Experience

Creators should not feel like they are merely uploading content.

CONUS should communicate:

> **Bring us something worth making.**

Creator journey:

**DISCOVER → PITCH → REVIEW → PILOT → RELEASE → MEASURE → DEVELOP → OPPORTUNITY**

---

## Creator landing experience

Explain:

* what CONUS is looking for
* what can be submitted
* how ideas are evaluated
* what happens after submission
* what creators can gain

---

## Pitch experience

Submission should capture:

* creator
* concept
* format
* audience
* why it matters
* 60-second pitch
* sample material

Avoid unnecessarily long forms.

The creator should understand what happens after submission.

---

# 18. The Founder Experience

CONUS Founders should feel like a public opportunity system.

Journey:

**APPLY → VERIFY → PROFILE → DISCOVER → COMPETE → VOTE → FINAL → OPPORTUNITY**

A Founder profile should communicate:

* who they are
* what they are building
* what problem they address
* what makes them interesting
* what they need
* why the audience should support them

The audience should be able to understand the person quickly.

---

# 19. Voting UX

Voting must communicate trust.

Before voting:

* what is being voted on
* who is eligible
* when voting closes
* how many votes are allowed
* what the vote affects

After voting:

> **Your vote has been counted.**

Then:

> Voting closes in 4h 21m.

Where appropriate:

> **Share this competition**

The system should not reveal sensitive vote information prematurely if doing so could distort participation.

---

# 20. Challenge UX

Challenges should make the assignment extremely clear.

A challenge page should communicate:

### What

What must be done?

### Who

Who can participate?

### When

When does it close?

### Why

What can participants gain?

### How

How is it judged?

### What

What happens afterward?

The primary action should be obvious:

**ENTER CHALLENGE**

# 21. Content Experience

Content should always have context.

A video should not simply be:

> Video

It should communicate:

* what it is
* why it matters
* where it sits in CONUS
* whether it is part of a series
* what action comes next

Example:

> **THE LAST 500**
>
> Episode 4 of 6
>
> Watch → Discuss → Vote

# 23. Discovery UX

Discovery should not feel like an infinite content dump.

Organize discovery around meaningful questions:

### What's happening?

Current activity.

### What's worth watching?

Stories and shows.

### Who should I know?

People, creators, founders.

### What's trending?

Topics and cultural momentum.

### What's happening near me?

Local.

### What can I participate in?

Challenges, competitions, voting.

This creates purposeful discovery.

# 24. Trends UX

CONUS Trends should reveal movement.

Not simply:

> Most viewed.

But potentially:

* rising
* gaining attention
* heavily discussed
* rapidly shared
* growing locally
* crossing communities

The experience should communicate:

> **Something is happening here.**

---

# 25. Following

Following should create a personalized reason to return.

Users may follow:

* people
* creators
* founders
* shows
* topics
* locations

Following should generate meaningful updates.

Examples:

> A new episode from a show you follow.

> A creator you follow entered a challenge.

> Your city moved up one position.

> A founder you support reached the final.

The goal is not notification volume.

The goal is **relevance**.

---

# 26. Notifications

Notifications should be event-driven.

Good notification:

> **Your city just moved to #2.**

Weak notification:

> “Come back to CONUS!”

Good:

> **The creator you follow just released Episode 3.**

Weak:

> “New content available.”

Good:

> **Voting closes in 30 minutes.**

Notifications should tell people **why now**.

---

# 27. Rewards UX

Rewards must be understandable.

Every reward experience should explain:

* what the reward is
* who qualifies
* how eligibility works
* when it is determined
* how it is delivered
* whether verification is required

Avoid creating uncertainty around money.

# 36. Empty States

Empty states should be useful, not dead.

Instead of:

> No results.

Use:

> **Nothing here yet.**
>
> Try exploring creators, stories, or topics.

Or:

> **No active challenges right now.**
>
> See what's happening today.

Every empty state should offer a next action.

---

# 37. Loading States

Loading should preserve context.

Instead of generic:

> Loading...

Use contextual language where appropriate:

> Finding today's results...

> Loading the leaderboard...

> Preparing your challenge...

The interface should feel active without becoming theatrical for no reason.

---

# 38. Error States

Errors should explain:

1. What happened.
2. Whether the user's action was saved.
3. What they should do next.

Example:

> **Something went wrong.**
>
> Your submission was not completed.
>
> **Try again**

For payments/rewards:

> **We couldn't confirm your payment.**
>
> No charge was confirmed.
>
> **Try again**

Trust depends heavily on this.

---

# 39. Connectivity and Low-Bandwidth UX

CONUS is intended for a mobile-first environment where connectivity may vary.

The UX should prioritize:

* fast loading
* lightweight pages
* compressed media
* progressive loading
* minimal unnecessary animation
* resilient forms
* clear loading states
* graceful failure
* shareable URLs
* PWA behavior

The core experience should remain usable under imperfect connectivity.

---

# 40. Mobile-First Interaction

Primary interactions should be designed around:

* thumb reach
* vertical scrolling
* large touch targets
* minimal typing
* fast decisions
* one-handed use where possible

Avoid desktop-first information density.

The mobile interface should feel native even though CONUS is initially a web/PWA experience.

# 50. UX Hierarchy

The product hierarchy should generally follow:

### Level 1 — Moment

What is happening now?

### Level 2 — Experience

What can I watch, play, vote on, or enter?

### Level 3 — Context

Who, what, where, why?

### Level 4 — Position

How am I doing?

### Level 5 — Identity

What does my history say about me?

### Level 6 — Opportunity

What can happen because I participated?

This creates a progression from immediate entertainment to long-term value.

# 57. The UX Success Test

A feature should be considered successful when a participant can understand:

1. **What is happening?**
2. **Why should I care?**
3. **What can I do?**
4. **What happened because I did it?**
5. **What can I do next?**

If a feature cannot answer these questions, its UX should be reconsidered.

4. Featured story/show
5. Local activity
6. Recognition
7. Discovery

Not everything needs equal visual weight.

The current moment wins.

---

# 61. CONUS Zero Core User Flow

The primary proof loop:

**OPEN**

↓

**SEE TODAY'S CONUS**

↓

**START**

↓

**PARTICIPATE**

↓

**RESULT**

↓

**RANK**

↓

**SHARE**

↓

**DISCOVER NEXT EXPERIENCE**

↓

**RETURN TOMORROW**

If this loop does not work, additional features should not be prioritized.

---

# 62. The "Next" Principle

Every meaningful completion should create a next possibility.

Examples:

Quiz complete:

> See leaderboard

Leaderboard:

> Challenge your city

Story complete:

> Watch another

Episode complete:

> Follow show

Creator discovered:

> See their work

Founder viewed:

> Support Founder

Challenge completed:

> See results

Result shared:

> Invite someone

The system should continuously create momentum.

---

# 63. The "Don't Trap Me" Principle

The user should never feel forced into a funnel.

Do not:

* block basic discovery behind registration
* force subscriptions before value
* force sharing
* force referrals
* force sponsor interactions
* make every action competitive
* overload people with notifications

Participation should feel earned by value.

---

# 64. The "Don't Confuse Me" Principle

CONUS has many systems.

The UX must hide unnecessary complexity.

The user should not need to understand:

* the full CONUS business model
* campaign architecture
* reward economics
* editorial operations
* internal classifications
* product ontology

They simply experience:

> **What's happening → What can I do → What happened → What's next**

---

# 65. The "Don't Waste My Time" Principle

Interactions should be:

* fast
* clear
* purposeful
* lightweight

Especially:

* quizzes
* polls
* voting
* challenges
* onboarding
* sharing

The user should always know the expected commitment.

Example:

> **5 questions · 60 seconds**

is better than:

> **Start Quiz**

---

# 66. The "Make It Worth It" Principle

Every important action should return something.

It might return:

* entertainment
* knowledge
* status
* recognition
* belonging
* reward
* opportunity
* discovery

The return does not always have to be financial.

---

# 67. The CONUS Experience Equation

A useful working model:

**Curiosity + Clarity + Action + Feedback + Meaning = Participation**

Then:

**Participation + Signal + Recognition = Opportunity**

And:

**Opportunity + Entertainment + Community = Long-term CONUS value**

---

# 68. UX Guardrails

CONUS should avoid:

* endless-feed behavior without purpose
* engagement bait
* artificial scarcity
* pay-to-win mechanics
* unclear voting
* hidden reward rules
* spam notifications
* sponsor interference with editorial judgment
* popularity being presented as merit
* ranking people without meaningful context
* confusing competition mechanics
* unnecessary registration barriers
* feature overload

---

# 69. The Human Rule

The system should assist human choice rather than replace it.

Algorithms may help surface:

* trends
* recommendations
* rising creators
* relevant challenges
* local activity

But CONUS should preserve:

* editorial judgment
* creator agency
* audience choice
* transparent rules
* human recognition
* meaningful opportunity

The product should never imply that an algorithm alone determines cultural value.

> **CONUS should make discovering something interesting feel like the beginning of an experience, participating feel meaningful, results feel consequential, recognition feel earned, and every meaningful action create a reason to return.**

# 72. Final UX Principle

CONUS should not feel like software people have to learn.

It should feel like **something happening that people want to be part of.**

The interface is only the mechanism.

The actual product experience is:

**Something is happening.**

**You noticed it.**

**You joined it.**

**Your participation mattered.**

**Something happened because you participated.**

**Now there is something else worth doing.**

That is the CONUS experience.
